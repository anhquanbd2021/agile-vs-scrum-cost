How scoring works

Two independent axes

Agile is the why — a mindset of continuous feedback, collaboration, and responding to change. Scrum is one how — a framework of roles, events, and artifacts. The detector scores them separately because the whole failure mode it hunts is the case where they diverge: every ceremony present, every behavior absent.

Ceremony checks — did the meeting happen?

  • sprint planning — does planning actually run each sprint?
  • daily standup — does the team sync every day?
  • sprint review — is there a demo of a working increment?
  • retrospective — does the team inspect itself each sprint?
  • roles staffed — a named Product Owner and Scrum Master?
  • backlog estimated — an ordered, estimated backlog?
  • fixed cadence — sprints of fixed length?

Mindset checks — did anything change?

  • scope renegotiable — can sprint scope bend when the team learns?
  • retro action shipped — did the last retro change a real rule?
  • velocity = forecast — is velocity for planning, not pressure?
  • PO decides — single priority authority, no committee?
  • real feedback — stakeholders whose input reorders the backlog?
  • done = usable — usable increment, not "merged"?
  • team sets pace — the team pulls to its own capacity?

The four quadrants

  • agile — Scrum done well — ≥5 ceremonies, ≥5 mindset.
  • agile without Scrum — <5 ceremonies, ≥5 mindset. Kanban, XP, or continuous delivery can carry the why.
  • zombie Scrum — ≥5 ceremonies, <5 mindset. The expensive case.
  • ad hoc — <5 on both. Neither the framework nor the mindset.

Honest limits

This is a structural checklist over self-reported inputs. It cannot tell an honest "yes" from an aspirational one, and a 7/7 mindset score does not prove the feedback loops produce good decisions — only that they exist. A team's real culture is observed, not declared. Use the scores to start a conversation at the next retro, not to end one.
